# JLG Manual Discography Verification 04 — *Ojalá que llueva café*

**Research date:** 2026-08-11  
**Mode:** album-by-album editorial review; production read-only  
**Identity rule:** MusicBrainz identifiers were not used to decide Recording identity.

## Album summary

**Verified album:** *Ojalá que llueva café*  
**Best-supported original public release:** 1989, Dominican Republic, Karen Records KLP-126  
**Original format/program:** eight-track 33⅓ RPM LP, 31:42  
**Original order:** Visa para un sueño; Ojalá que llueva café; Razones; De tu boca; La gallera; Woman del Callao; Reina mía; Ángel para una tambora.

The controlling original-media evidence is the [Archivo General de la Nación (AGN) catalog record](https://colecciones.agn.gob.do/opac/ficha.php?codopac=OUAUD&idpag=0459332462&informatico=00269538SR). It identifies the Dominican 1989 Karen KLP-126 LP, its eight recordings, order, format, and running time. The [official artist history](https://juanluisguerra.com/historia/) also places the album's production and the promotion of “Ojalá,” “Visa,” and “Woman del Callao” in 1989, although its compact discography list displays 1990. For original-release identity, the dated Dominican archival LP record prevails: **1989**, not 1990.

AGN credits Juan Luis Guerra as singer and arranger; Maridalia Hernández, Roger Zayas-Bazán, and Mariela Mercado as singers; and Adalberto Álvarez among the contributors. Guerra wrote the words/music except “Woman del Callao,” composed by Julio Delgado. These facts were documented only; no production credits were added.

**Recommended eventual action:** retain the original and each verified concert performance; eventually reassign the 2005 Track away from the 191.760-second bonus Recording after dependency review; align full release-specific audio before merging either uncertain row.  
**Notes:** no current Visa row is a safe merge candidate in this pass.

## Platform, dependency, and production-safety observations

Platform associations are notably unreliable in this album family. Several live rows point to the original Apple/Spotify/Amazon/Tidal audio; the 2021 Ojalá row points to a *Versión Privé* object instead of its actual *Entre Mar y Palmeras* object; the 2024 “La gallera” row mostly points to the 1989 audio; and the 404-second “De tu boca” row points to the ordinary album object. These conflicts were recorded, not cleaned.

The proposed redundant 2013 Ojalá row has two Tracks and shares the official performance object with the four-Track survivor row. Before any future merge, re-inventory Tracks, platform links, ISRCs, credits, sources, works, and analytics. The Visa `c8b6b240-...` problem is not a merge: it is a future Track-to-Recording correction because one Recording presently serves two independently distinct live performances.

Production remained read-only. No Recording, Track, Release, title, disambiguation, platform link, ISRC, credit, Work, `recordings.release_id`, schema object, RPC, search behavior, or public UX was changed.

## Duplication pattern compared with *Mudanza y acarreo*

The broad one-row-per-Spanish-reissue duplication pattern found on *Mudanza y acarreo* **does not recur**. The original eight studio rows are already heavily reused correctly across editions and compilations. The one new merge candidate is a duplicate import of a **2013 live performance**, not an album-edition duplicate. Most additional same-title rows are legitimate concert or later-version audio. This album demonstrates both sides of the architecture: extensive Track reuse of one studio Recording and separate Recording rows for genuinely different performances.
