# JLG Manual Discography Verification 06 — *Areíto*

**Research date:** 2026-08-11  
**Mode:** album-by-album editorial review; production read-only  
**Identity rule:** MusicBrainz identifiers were not used to determine Recording identity.

## Album summary

**Canonical title:** *Areíto*  
**Identity:** sixth Juan Luis Guerra/4.40 studio album; the next studio album after *Bachata Rosa*  
**Best-supported original public release:** 8 December 1992, Karen Records; contemporary-market catalog BMG/Karen 3456 and Karen CD 146 are independently documented  
**Phonogram year:** 1992  
**Original program:** twelve tracks, approximately 46:19–46:26  
**Original order:** Areíto; El costo de la vida; Señales de humo; Ayer; Frío frío; Rompiendo fuente; Mal de amor; Si saliera petróleo; Coronita de flores; Cuando te beso; Cuando te beso (bonus track); Naboria/Daca Mayanimacaná.

The [official artist history](https://juanluisguerra.com/historia/) identifies *Areíto* as the 1992 follow-up and highlights its social content. Contemporary Billboard-derived release reporting supports 8 December 1992 in the United States and describes the large Karen/BMG initial shipment; later artist biographies sometimes report 15 December. [Apple](https://music.apple.com/us/album/are%C3%ADto/1526561393), [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/album/0dMH2uVudqy6c3lm7Jynh8), and [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/areito-juan-luis-guerra-440/i0unam58p2jma) independently establish the 1992 phonogram, Karen label family, twelve-track sequence, and running time. Apple's `1 January` is a catalog placeholder rather than stronger evidence for the exact public date.

No Dominican AGN catalog record for this album was located in the targeted search. Physical-market evidence identifies Karen/BMG 3456 and Karen CD 146; a documented 1992 German Karen/Ariola/BMG CD is `74321 12897-2`. The three Mangulina editions have the same twelve-track program. Title variations include *Areito* without the accent, “Rompiendo Fuentes,” and “Cuando te beso II” versus the officially presented “Cuando Te Beso (Bonus Track).” These are metadata/presentation variations, not additional album tracks.

The two “Cuando te beso” positions are separate original-session Recordings, not duplicate Tracks: Apple exposes different official objects, durations, titles, and audio previews for the 3:29 main version and 3:11 bonus performance.

### Credits and arrangement evidence

Physical/liner-derived documentation identifies Juan Luis Guerra as producer and arranger/conductor; Bienvenido Rodríguez as executive producer; Sammy Velázquez as recording/mix engineer; and 440 Studios and Clinton Recording Studios in New York as recording locations. Documented performers include Juan Colón, Luis Aquino, Antonio Vásquez, Elvis Cabrera, Pedro Peralta, Rafael Martínez, Isidro Bobadilla, Johnny Chocolate, Marco Hernández, Adalgisa Pantaleón, and Roger Zayas-Bazán. Diblo Dibala's composition/guitar context underlies “El costo de la vida”; José Antonio Molina arranged/directed the two “Cuando te beso” orchestral presentations. These were documented only, not inserted into production.

Official previews differ, but were not reliably aligned enough to specify rerecording versus edit; the separate collection delivery is therefore retained conservatively. Romeo Santos) [Live].” The 216.548-second Apple object is the music-video presentation of that same concert performance; `8b866647-...` and `eaaf538f-...` share the exact Apple `715574224`, Spotify `1UzxNNiJhUPngbfANRlQqM`, and YouTube `EzI-Pcajixg` objects.

The 3:55 concert audio and 3:36 music-video edit are the same documented Romeo Santos concert performance in different edits, so remain different Recording presentations under the project's edit rule; the two rows representing the 3:36 edit are duplicates of each other.  
**Version/disambiguation:** original studio; **Colección romántica version**; **Asondeguerra Tour 2013 live featuring Romeo Santos (full audio)**; **2013 live music-video edit featuring Romeo Santos**; **Entre Mar y Palmeras 2021 medley**.

**Current `recordings.release_id` assessment:** original points to 1995 *Grandes éxitos*; collection row points to *Colección romántica*; full concert row points to the 2013 stadium tour; video rows point to tour/video single contexts.  
**Platform observations:** original and collection rows carry contaminated Spotify/Apple associations to 2013 audio in places. Release-specific Apple objects were used instead.  
**Classification:** original KEEP — ORIGINAL / UNIQUE RECORDING; collection KEEP — NEEDS DISAMBIGUATION; full concert and video edit KEEP — DISTINCT VERSION; `eaaf538f-...` SAME RECORDING — MERGE CANDIDATE into video-edit survivor `8b866647-...`; medley KEEP — DISTINCT VERSION.  
**Confidence:** HIGH except exact collection mechanism, MEDIUM/UNCERTAIN.  
**Recommended eventual action:** retain original, collection presentation, full concert, one video-edit row, and medley; consolidate the one-Track duplicate edit after dependency review.  
**Notes:** do not merge the 3:36 video edit into the 3:55 full concert audio; they are different edits of one performance.

## Historical duplication pattern

The broad Spanish-reissue duplication pattern from *Mudanza y acarreo* does **not** repeat: all three *Areíto* editions use the same twelve original/session Recording UUIDs. New duplication is limited to two one-Track collection imports and one duplicate row for the same 2013 music-video edit. This continues the post-*Mudanza* pattern: studio-album reuse is mostly correct, while actual duplication occurs at compilation/collection and alternate-version import boundaries.
