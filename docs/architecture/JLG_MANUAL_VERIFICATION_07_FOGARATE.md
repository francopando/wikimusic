# JLG Manual Discography Verification 07 — *Fogaraté!*

**Research date:** 2026-08-11  
**Mode:** album-by-album editorial review; production read-only  
**Identity rule:** MusicBrainz identifiers were not used to determine Recording identity.

## Album summary

**Canonical title:** *Fogaraté!* (the exclamation mark is original physical-release styling; current digital services commonly display *Fogaraté*)  
**Identity:** seventh Juan Luis Guerra/4.40 studio album; the next studio album after *Areíto*  
**Best-supported original public release:** 19 July 1994  
**Phonogram year:** 1994  
**Release context:** a Dominican Juan Luis Guerra/4.40 production issued by Karen and distributed internationally through BMG-related channels  
**Original program:** twelve studio tracks, approximately 46:06–46:09  
**Original order:** Los Mangos Bajitos; El Farolito; Los Pajaritos; Viviré; El Beso de la Ciguatera; Oficio de Enamorado; Lacrimosa; Fogaraté; Canto de Hacha; Oprobio; July 19th; La Cosquillita.

The [official artist history](https://juanluisguerra.com/historia/) identifies *Fogaraté* as the 1994 follow-up and “La Cosquillita” as its lead vehicle, explicitly connecting it to Dominican *perico ripiao*. The independent [Amoeba physical CD record](https://www.amoeba.com/fogarat-cd-juan-luis-guerra/albums/236012/) documents a 1994 BMG Canada CD, catalog `CDK-165`, 46:06, with the complete twelve-track sequence. [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/fogarat%C3%A9-mw0000116175) independently gives the same 1994 program and 46:06 duration, documents an International Music issue `21110` and a 1995 Universal Music Latino cassette `930 165`, and supplies track-level composition and performance credits. The [official Apple album](https://music.apple.com/us/album/fogarat%C3%A9/1526554412) confirms twelve songs, 46 minutes, and ℗ 1994 Karen Publishing Company. The exact 19 July date is supported by contemporary-release indexing and the date-specific album catalog; Apple's `1 January` is a catalog placeholder, not contrary exact-date evidence.

No Dominican AGN catalog record was located in the targeted search. The evidence establishes contemporary CD and cassette circulation and later digital delivery, but does not securely establish one exclusive “first country” or one exclusive original format. All independently checked editions retain the same twelve-song program. The punctuation/diacritic forms *Fogaraté!*, *Fogaraté*, and *Fogarate* are title presentation differences, not editions or Recordings. One current Spotify album presentation has shown “Estrellitas y Duendes” at position 2, contrary to the physical program and the Karen/Apple/Amazon program; that is treated as a platform metadata mismatch, not a supported historical track substitution.

### Credits and arrangement evidence

Physical/catalog evidence identifies Juan Luis Guerra as principal artist, composer and guitarist. The documented personnel include Diblo Dibala (composer/guitar), Francisco Ulloa (accordion, vocals and composer), Eddy Montalvo (congas), Juan Colón (saxophone), Barry Danielian (trumpet), Jorge González (bongos/cencerro/maracas), Oscar Hernández (piano), Isidro Bobadilla (percussion), Mario Rivera (baritone/flute), Manuel Tejada (programming), David “Piro” Rodríguez (trumpet), and additional 4.40/session players. These credits were documented only.

AllMusic's track credits independently support Juan Antonio Alix/Diblo Dibala/Juan Luis Guerra for “Los Mangos Bajitos,” Papa Wemba/Guerra for “Viviré,” Dibala/Guerra for “Fogaraté,” Francisco Ulloa for “Canto de Hacha,” Rafael Hernández for “Oprobio,” and Guerra/Ulloa for “La Cosquillita.” Its review and the physical credits establish the album's soukous and accordion-led típico contexts. “Lacrimosa” is the album's bachata treatment of the Mozart *Requiem* movement. These are Work/arrangement/credit facts, not evidence that compilation appearances are new Recordings.

## Arrangement review

The original album contains independently documented arrangement/adaptation contexts: soukous-derived material and guitar work involving Diblo Dibala; accordion-led *perico ripiao* involving Francisco Ulloa; the Guerra/Papa Wemba “Viviré” source relationship; the bachata treatment of Mozart's “Lacrimosa”; and the Rafael Hernández “Oprobio.” These should eventually be modeled at Work/Arrangement/credit level.

For cross-Recording comparison, the 2021 “El Farolito” and 2005 “La Cosquillita” are proven different performances. Available evidence does not justify counting either as a new Arrangement merely because it is live, longer/shorter, or differently instrumented in concert. Accordingly, **zero new distinct arrangements** are counted in this album review.

## Historical duplication pattern

The broad Spanish-reissue duplication pattern from *Mudanza y acarreo* does **not** repeat. Both Mangulina *Fogaraté!* editions reuse the same twelve original/session UUIDs. The actual duplicate pattern is the narrower one already seen after *Mudanza*: one-Track rows imported at *The Collection* boundaries. Alternate-version architecture is also mostly sound: the 2021 live “El Farolito” and the 2005 live “La Cosquillita” are separate rows, although the latter's links are contaminated by the studio master.
